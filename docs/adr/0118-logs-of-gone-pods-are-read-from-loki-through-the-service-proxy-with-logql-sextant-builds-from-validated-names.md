# ADR-0118: Logs of gone pods are read from Loki through the service proxy, with LogQL Sextant builds from validated names

## Context

#265: once a pod is deleted (or its node rotated the log files), the kubelet has nothing left to stream, and "Previous container" covers only the run just before the current one. Clusters that keep their logs usually ship them to Grafana Loki. Loki's HTTP API takes free-form LogQL, label names and time ranges, and can answer with very large bodies; a tenant header decides what a multi-tenant installation shows.

## Decision

- **Found like Prometheus, never installed.** `crates/sextant-core/src/loki.rs` lists the Services of the namespaces `loki`, `logging`, `monitoring`, `observability`, `grafana`, `loki-stack` and, cluster-wide, those labelled `app.kubernetes.io/name in (loki,loki-distributed)` (≤ 100 each), and keeps the names the charts' templates give: grafana/loki (`production/helm/loki`) `<fullname>-gateway` on 80, `<fullname>-query-frontend` and `<fullname>-read` on 3100, the single binary `<fullname>` on 3100; grafana/loki-distributed the same gateway and query frontend; grafana/loki-stack's Loki `<release>` on 3100 (`<fullname>` is `loki`, `<release>-loki`, `loki-distributed` or `<release>-loki-distributed`). The best of them (candidate namespaces in order, then gateway → query frontend → read → single binary, ≤ 6 probed) that answers `/loki/api/v1/status/buildinfo` (Loki 2.3 on, passed on by the gateway), else `/ready`, is Loki. The cluster settings (`ClusterSettings.loki`, frontend-owned, read leniently) may name the Service, port and path prefix instead, or turn Loki off, and may give a tenant sent as `X-Scope-OrgID` (letters, digits, `!-_.*'()`, ≤ 150, several joined by `|`). No URL is accepted: only a Service reached through the API server.
- **Only through the service proxy**, with the user's own credentials, as Prometheus (ADR-0102) and OpenCost (ADR-0087): no local port, nothing held between calls, RBAC on `services/proxy` decides. Detection and the label scheme are reused for 60 s per connection and settings value; a Loki that refuses the labels (no tenant: "no org id"; no permission) is an error that says so, not "not found".
- **A fixed set of label schemes.** `/loki/api/v1/labels` (last 24 h) decides which one: `namespace`/`pod`/`container` (Promtail's and Grafana Alloy's Kubernetes relabelling) or `k8s_namespace_name`/`k8s_pod_name`/`k8s_container_name` (OpenTelemetry resource attributes as Loki's OTLP ingestion indexes them). Without namespace and pod labels nothing is read and the window says why; without the container label a pod's containers are read together and the window says so.
- **Sextant builds every request.** The window sends names (namespace, pod, container; or a workload's namespace, kind and name), a range in milliseconds and a line limit; `validate_*` checks them (DNS-1123 names, kinds Deployment/ReplicaSet/StatefulSet/DaemonSet/Job/CronJob, 1 ms–24 h ending at most 5 minutes ahead, 1–5000 lines) before the cluster is looked up. The LogQL is only a stream selector, `{<ns>="…", <pod>="…" | <pod>=~"…"[, <container>="…"]}`, values escaped as LogQL strings. A workload's previous pods come from `/loki/api/v1/label/<pod label>/values` with an anchored pattern of the names its controller gives (`metrics/prometheus/rightsizing.rs`'s safe alphabet; CronJob pods through their Jobs `<name>-<minutes>-<5>`), ≤ 200 names; a pod's containers from the container label's values.
- **Caps.** 30 s and 8 MiB per request (a bigger answer is refused, not cut), ≤ 24 h and ≤ 5000 lines per read in pages of 1000, ≤ 16 MiB of line text per read, ≤ 14 requests per read; lines longer than 64 KiB are sent in pieces marked `cut`, like live logs. Paging follows `logcli`: the next request starts at the last timestamp sent (forward) or ends one nanosecond after it (backward, `end` excluded), and the lines with that timestamp already sent are dropped from its answer.
- **A stream like live logs.** `loki_logs_start` takes a stream id and a channel (`openStream`, ADR-0078), registers the read in `AppState.subscriptions` (stopped by `loki_logs_stop`, disconnecting, `streams_reset`, closing the window) and sends pages oldest first, then `ended` with the count and whether the limit stopped it.
- **In the logs view.** "Load older lines from Loki" (last 1 h / 6 h / 24 h / custom ≤ 24 h) is open at once when the pod is gone, behind a button otherwise. The read ends where the run the live view shows began (the container's `startedAt`, the previous run's with "Previous container"), or now for a gone pod, so its lines precede the live ones; with "Timestamps" on, a Loki line with the same timestamp and text as one of the oldest 2000 live lines is dropped. Loki's lines carry a "Loki" mark, are searched and saved with the live ones, and take the kubelet's timestamp format. A workload's details list "Logs of previous pods (Loki)" on request; a name opens a tab of Loki's lines only (no live stream). Without Loki both places say what Sextant looked for and where to name the Service.
- **Read-only, window only.** `loki_status`, `loki_names`, `loki_logs_start`, `loki_logs_stop` change nothing; MCP agents and extensions get no Loki tool or method (test in `src-tauri/src/commands/loki.rs`).

## Why

- Passing LogQL from the window was rejected: line filters, parsers and metric queries can make Loki scan a whole retention period, and a fixed selector keeps each request reviewable and bounded.
- A port-forward or a typed URL was rejected for the same reasons as for Prometheus and OpenCost: a held port, and a URL that could reach anything.
- Interleaving Loki's lines with the live buffer by time was rejected: the live view holds up to 100 000 lines in chunks; ending the read where the shown run began keeps the two apart by construction, and the oldest live lines are enough to drop the overlap at the boundary.

## Consequences

- A Loki behind its own authentication (basic auth, an OAuth proxy in front of the gateway) cannot be read: Sextant sends no credentials of its own.
- Without "Timestamps" a Loki line equal to a live line at the boundary may show twice.
- Label schemes other than the two are "no labels Sextant knows" until added here with a fixture.

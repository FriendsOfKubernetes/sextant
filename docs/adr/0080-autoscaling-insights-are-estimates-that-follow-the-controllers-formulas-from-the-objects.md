# ADR-0080: Autoscaling insights are estimates that follow the controllers' formulas, read from the objects

## Context

"Why did it not scale?" (#146) has several layers. A HorizontalPodAutoscaler may be missing metrics, held by its tolerance, clamped by `maxReplicas`, limited by its behavior or stabilizing; a new pod may stay Pending because no node fits; KEDA may manage the HPA through a ScaledObject; Karpenter or Cluster Autoscaler may or may not add a node, and may remove one for drift, consolidation or emptiness. The facts are in the objects (HPA `status` and conditions, events, the scheduler's FailedScheduling message, KEDA and Karpenter conditions, Cluster Autoscaler's status ConfigMap), but each is written in its controller's own terms. Sextant cannot run the controllers, and the metrics they read are not all reachable (custom and external metrics APIs, the controllers' in-memory history).

## Decision

- **Read-only and frontend only.** Pure engines in `src/features/autoscaling/` (`hpa.ts`, `scheduling.ts`, `keda.ts`, `karpenter.ts`, `clusterAutoscaler.ts`) over watched objects, with table-driven tests; no Rust command, nothing written to the cluster, nothing for agents or extensions.
- **The HPA formulas are the controller's** (`pkg/controller/podautoscaler`: `horizontal.go`, `replica_calculator.go`). Per metric `ratio = current / target` (Utilization against `averageUtilization`, AverageValue, Value) proposes `ceil(currentReplicas × ratio)`, or no change while `|ratio − 1| ≤ tolerance` (0.1, or `behavior.scaleUp/scaleDown.tolerance` where the cluster supports it); the largest proposal wins; when a metric has no value and the others would scale down, nothing changes; the result is clamped to `[minReplicas, maxReplicas]` and to one step of the behavior's policies (`selectPolicy` Max/Min/Disabled, the default policies when none is set, as `calculateScaleUpLimitWithScalingRules` / `calculateScaleDownLimitWithBehaviors`). Changing a formula means checking it against `horizontal.go` again; the function names are in the comments.
- **It is labelled an estimate.** The controller divides by ready pods for resource metrics and stabilizes over a history it keeps in memory; the window has neither, so the estimate uses the replica count, and stabilization is explained from the `AbleToScale`/`ScalingLimited` conditions, never recomputed. Conditions, FailedGet*Metric messages (no metrics API, a container without a request — injected sidecars included —, pods without metrics) and the target's container requests for Utilization targets are turned into plain sentences.
- **Scheduling is the scheduler's message first, a local check second.** The FailedScheduling message (and Cluster Autoscaler's NotTriggerScaleUp list) is parsed into reasons with node counts. The local check runs the default scheduler's filters against watched nodes (cordon, nodeSelector and required node affinity, NoSchedule/NoExecute taints, allocatable minus the requests of bound pods with the sidecar and init-container rules, required pod (anti-)affinity and DoNotSchedule topology spread in the pod's namespace, bound PersistentVolumes' node affinity), at most 200 nodes, and lists what it does not judge (other pods' anti-affinity, host ports, volume limits, namespaceSelector of affinity terms, DRA claims).
- **Node autoscalers are read from what they publish.** Karpenter from NodePool limits against `status.resources`, NodeClaim conditions, disruption conditions, taints, the `karpenter.sh/do-not-disrupt` annotation and events; Cluster Autoscaler from `kube-system/cluster-autoscaler-status` (YAML from 1.30, the older text before), its events and node taints, read only after it wrote an event about the pod; KEDA from ScaledObject/ScaledJob conditions, pause annotations and the managed `keda-hpa-<name>`. TriggerAuthentications are summarised by where their parameters come from, never by value. Their sidebar sections are discovered sections, shown only while the cluster serves the kinds.
- **Bounded data.** Nodes and the requests of bound pods are taps on the problems store's cluster-wide Node and Pod watches (`acquireRows`, compact rows, held 60 s after the last view); namespace lists and Karpenter lists are the resource map's shared watches; an object's events are plain watches that end with the view.

## Why

- Asking the metrics APIs (`custom.metrics.k8s.io`, `external.metrics.k8s.io`) ourselves was rejected: the HPA status already holds the current value the controller used, and a second read at another moment would disagree with the decision being explained.
- A simplified "current / target" ratio was rejected: tolerance, missing-metric holding and behavior limits are exactly the cases people ask about.
- Simulating the scheduler fully (scoring, preemption, all plugins) was rejected: the scheduler's message is authoritative; the local check only points at the nodes and filters, and says what it skips.

## Consequences

- The numbers can differ from the controller's next decision by the ready-pod count and the stabilization window; the view says "estimate" and shows the controller's own conditions next to it.
- A new Kubernetes release that changes the HPA algorithm (a new behavior field, tolerance defaults) needs `hpa.ts` and its tests updated against `horizontal.go`.
- Clusters without metrics-server or with an autoscaler Sextant does not know get the HPA's own conditions and the scheduler's message, without the tool-specific views.

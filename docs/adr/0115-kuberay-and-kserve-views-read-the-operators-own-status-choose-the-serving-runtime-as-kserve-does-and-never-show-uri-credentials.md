# ADR-0115: KubeRay and KServe views read the operators' own status, choose the serving runtime as KServe does, and never show URI credentials

## Context

#248 asked for KubeRay (`ray.io` RayCluster, RayJob, RayService) and #249 for KServe (`serving.kserve.io` InferenceService, ServingRuntime, ClusterServingRuntime, InferenceGraph, TrainedModel) in the style of the other CRD projects (ADR-0097, 0100, 0101): a discovered sidebar section, a status column, details that explain a failure from the objects' own status, problems on the Problems screen that agents never get, fixtures from the projects' examples, strings in every language, axe tests. Read-only.

Both projects changed their status over time. KubeRay's `RayCluster.status.state` (ready / suspended / failed) is deprecated in favour of conditions (`RayClusterProvisioned`, `HeadPodReady`, `ReplicaFailure`, `RayClusterSuspending`, `RayClusterSuspended`; behind the `RayClusterStatusConditions` gate from 1.2, on by default from 1.3), and `failed` is no longer written; a RayService's `serviceStatus` ("Running" or empty) is deprecated in favour of the `Ready` and `UpgradeInProgress` conditions (1.3). KServe renamed the deployment modes (`Serverless` → `Knative`, `RawDeployment` → `Standard`, `ParseDeploymentMode` in pkg/constants) and changed the default to Standard; newer releases record the mode and the chosen runtime in the status. A KServe storage URI can carry credentials: `https://user:token@host/…`, a pre-signed `?X-Amz-Signature=…` or an Azure SAS query.

Sources read: github.com/ray-project/kuberay `ray-operator/apis/ray/v1/{raycluster,rayjob,rayservice}_types.go`, `controllers/ray/utils/constant.go` (pod labels), `controllers/ray/rayjob_controller.go` (`createNewK8sJob`: the submitter Job has the RayJob's own name), `controllers/ray/common/association.go` (`RayServiceServeServiceNamespacedName`), `config/samples/`; github.com/kserve/kserve `pkg/apis/serving/v1beta1/{inference_service_status,predictor,predictor_model,component}.go`, `pkg/apis/serving/v1alpha1/{servingruntime_types,inference_graph,trained_model}.go`, `pkg/constants/constants.go`, `pkg/controller/v1beta1/inferenceservice/components/predictor.go`, `pkg/controller/v1beta1/inferenceservice/utils/utils.go` (`GetServingRuntime`), `config/runtimes/`, `docs/samples/v1beta1/sklearn/`.

## Decision

- **Read-only, frontend only**: `src/features/kuberay/` and `src/features/kserve/`, discovered sidebar sections `kuberay` ("KubeRay") and `kserve` ("KServe"), each kind listed only where discovery serves it, generic Edit and Delete only. No Rust command; nothing is written. Strings: namespaces `kuberay` and `kserve`; Ray's "head", "worker" and "Serve" and KServe's component names stay as the projects name them.
- **KubeRay states from conditions first.** A RayCluster is Suspending / Suspended (condition, or the old state), Failed (`ReplicaFailure` True, or the old `state: failed`), Head not ready (provisioned once but `HeadPodReady` False), Ready (both True), else Provisioning; without conditions the old `state` decides. A RayJob is Retrying (`jobDeploymentStatus: Retrying`, before any failure: the last attempt's `FAILED` is not final), Failed (`Failed`, `ValidationFailed` or Ray's `FAILED`, which also covers operators before 1.3 that ended a failed job as `Complete`), Succeeded, Stopped, Suspended, Running or Pending. A RayService is Suspended, Failed (`Ready` False with `ValidationFailed` or `InitializingTimeout`, or a Serve application `DEPLOY_FAILED`/`UNHEALTHY` on the active cluster — or on the pending one while no active cluster exists yet; during an upgrade the active cluster still serves), Upgrading, Ready or Not ready; without conditions `serviceStatus: Running` means ready.
- **KubeRay links**: the head pod (with "Head logs", the existing single-pod logs tab), its Service, the RayJob's RayCluster and its submitter Job (`K8sJobMode` only, by the RayJob's own name) with "Submitter logs" (the merged logs of `job-name=<name>`), the RayService's active and pending RayClusters and its serve Service. A RayCluster's details list its pods by `ray.io/cluster=<name>` in its namespace — one label-selected watch through the shared registry, capped at `MAX_RAY_PODS` (2000; the view says when cut), at most 50 rows drawn — and count ready pods per worker group by `ray.io/group`; "Worker logs" opens the merged logs of `ray.io/cluster=<name>,ray.io/node-type=worker` (≤ 20 streams, the merged tab's own cap). The entrypoint and `serveConfigV2` are shown as text, cut at 20 000 characters; `runtimeEnvYAML` is not shown (it can hold environment values; the YAML section has it).
- **KServe states**: Stopped (the `serving.kserve.io/stop: "true"` annotation or the `Stopped` condition), Failed (the model failed to load — `FailedToLoad` in `modelStatus.states` or `transitionStatus: BlockedByFailedLoad` — or `InvalidSpec`), Ready / Not ready by the `Ready` condition, else Progressing. The explanation is the model's `lastFailureInfo` message, else the most basic False condition: components (`PredictorReady`, …) before configurations, the latest deployment, routes and the ingress, since a component that is not ready makes the routes and ingress not ready too.
- **The deployment mode** is `status.deploymentMode`, else the annotation, old names read as the new ones; neither set means "the cluster's default", which Sextant does not read (the `inferenceservice-config` ConfigMap). What KServe made for an InferenceService is found by its labels `serving.kserve.io/inferenceservice=<name>` and `component=<predictor|transformer|explainer>` (the object meta of `predictor.go`): Knative Services (linked into the Knative Serving section), Deployments and Services of its namespace, three metadata-only label-selected watches of served kinds, ≤ 100 each, closed with the details. Revisions and the traffic split (`status.components.<c>.traffic`, the canary share = the percent on `latestRevision` entries) link to Knative Revisions.
- **The serving runtime is chosen as KServe chooses it** (`runtimes.ts`, citing `GetSupportingRuntimes`, `RuntimeSupportsModel`, `sortServingRuntimeList`, `sortSupportedRuntimeByPriority`, `GetServingRuntime`): a runtime named in `model.runtime` is looked up as a ServingRuntime of the namespace, then a ClusterServingRuntime, and must be enabled, speak the model's protocol and list its format (auto-select not needed); otherwise the namespace's ServingRuntimes, then the ClusterServingRuntimes, each filtered (enabled, single-model unless ModelMesh, multi-node exactly when the predictor has a `workerSpec`, the model's protocol — `v1` when unset — and the format, with its version when the model names one, among auto-selectable formats), sorted by lowest protocol version, newest, name, then stably by the format's priority (a priority before none, higher first); the first wins. The details show the choice, the other candidates in order, why none fits, and a note when it differs from what KServe recorded in the status. The runtimes are two watches of served kinds (the namespace's and the cluster's), whole objects, ≤ 500 each, only while an InferenceService's details are open; an unreadable or cut list makes the choice "cannot be told", never a guess.
- **No credentials in any URI shown**: every URI the KServe views show (storage URIs, also from `storageUris` and a container's `STORAGE_URI`, status URLs and addresses, component URLs, InferenceGraph step URLs, TrainedModel storage) goes through `safeUri`: userinfo, query and fragment are dropped, and "credentials and query hidden" is said when anything was. The table's URL column shows the same text. The YAML section still shows the object as stored, as for any other kind.
- **Problems**, on by default, riding the problems store's per-kind watches: category `kuberay` — a RayCluster Failed by `ReplicaFailure` after 5 minutes (pod creation fails transiently), by the old `state: failed` at once, its head not ready after 5 minutes; a failed RayJob for a day after `endTime` (watch capped at `MAX_SOURCE_OBJECTS`, batch jobs can be thousands); a RayService not ready after 10 minutes (at once for `ValidationFailed`/`InitializingTimeout`), with a failed application's message. Category `kserve` — an InferenceService whose model failed to load at once, and one whose `Ready` is False after 10 minutes, with the failing condition's reason and message. Stopped, suspended and deleting objects have none. The table's "Only problems" filter has no grace and shows failed RayJobs however old. Both categories are in `NEVER_FOR_AGENTS` (with a test); no MCP tool or extension method was added.

## Why

- The operators' own status is what `kubectl get`/`ray`/`kserve` users read; re-deriving it from pods would disagree with the operator. Old fields are still read because clusters run old operators for years.
- Choosing the runtime is the question KServe users ask most ("why did it pick that runtime", "no runtime supports …"); answering it from the same rules, on the same objects, makes the answer KServe's.
- A storage URI is configuration that people paste credentials into; the details panel is shown on screen and in screenshots, so it must never print them, while the YAML section stays the deliberate place to see the stored object.

## Consequences

- The RayCluster pod list and the per-group ready counts come from one label-selected watch; a cluster with more than 2000 pods shows a cut list (the status counts stay whole).
- KServe's ModelMesh and LLMInferenceService kinds are not sidebar kinds; a ModelMesh InferenceService is shown, and its runtime chosen among multi-model runtimes, but ModelMesh's own placement is not explained.
- A cluster's default deployment mode and the KServe configuration (ingress domain, storage initializer) are not read; the details say "the cluster's default" rather than guess it.
- An InferenceService not ready for 10 minutes notifies even while a very large model is still downloading; the model status says so in the details.

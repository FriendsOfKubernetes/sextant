# ADR-0101: Knative, Crossplane and Kyverno views read status and references; Kyverno failures are an opt-in problem category

## Context

#213 asked for readable status of popular CRD projects in the style of the Flux, Argo CD and cert-manager sections. This ADR covers its Knative Serving, Crossplane and Kyverno parts (Argo Rollouts and Workflows: ADR-0097; External Secrets, CloudNativePG, Strimzi and Prometheus Operator: ADR-0100). Each project's state is in its objects' conditions and references, but each has a trap: an old Knative revision that failed stays failed forever; Crossplane's claim, composite and managed kinds are not fixed but defined at run time by XRDs and providers; Kyverno can report a failure for hundreds of objects at once, in one report per resource.

## Decision

- **Read-only, frontend only.** `src/features/knative/`, `src/features/crossplane/`, `src/features/kyverno/` add no Rust command; the kinds offer the generic Edit and Delete only. Each is a discovered sidebar section (`knative`, `crossplane`, `kyverno`) shown only for the kinds the cluster serves; nothing is watched before discovery.
- **Knative Serving.** Services, Configurations, Revisions, Routes and DomainMappings (`serving.knative.dev`) with a status column (ready / progressing / failed from Ready True / Unknown / False) and the failing condition's reason. Details explain the first False condition other than Ready (ConfigurationsReady, RoutesReady, ContainerHealthy, ResourcesAvailable…; `Active` False is a revision scaled to zero, never the failure), show the URL, the latest created and latest ready revision, and the traffic split from `status.traffic` (else `spec.traffic`, marked as asked) with links to the Revisions, and a Revision's actual / desired replicas.
  - **Problems** (category `knative`, on by default): a Service with Ready False, and a Configuration of its own (one a Service owns is told through the Service), after a 2-minute grace. Revisions are not watched: Knative reflects the latest created revision's failure in ConfigurationsReady, so only the latest revision's failure counts, once; an old revision that failed is history.
  - Amended (v0.20.0): the section is now titled "Knative Serving", beside a "Knative Eventing" section whose not-ready objects join the same `knative` category (ADR-0106).
- **Crossplane.** XRDs, Compositions, Providers, Configurations and Functions are registered kinds. Claim and composite kinds come from the XRDs (`spec.names`, `spec.claimNames`, the referenceable version), not from CRD categories: discovery carries no categories and reading every CRD would be megabytes. A managed resource is recognised by its shape (`spec.forProvider` or `spec.providerConfigRef`). Both layouts are read: v1 (`spec.resourceRef(s)`, `spec.claimRef`) and v2 (`spec.crossplane.*`).
  - **State:** paused when its reconciling is paused — `Synced` False with reason `ReconcilePaused` (crossplane-runtime's `ReconcilePaused()`, `apis/core/v2/condition.go`), written for the annotation `crossplane.io/paused: "true"` or management policies that pause it, or that annotation itself: a neutral state the user asked for, never failed and never a problem; failed when Synced is False (a reconcile error) or Ready is False for another reason than Creating / Deleting; progressing while creating, deleting or Unknown. The first failing condition is Synced first, then any other, Ready last; a pause is not one (amended after the 0.18 review).
  - **The chain** Claim → composite (`resourceRef`) → composed resources (`resourceRefs`) in the details panel: each named object read once with `resource_get` (at most 30, 4 at a time) and again when the shown object changes — not one watch per named object, since a composite can name dozens of managed resources of as many kinds. A managed resource links up to its composite (its controller owner reference).
  - **"Composite resources" page and problems** (category `crossplane`, on by default) share one store (`store.ts`): the XRD list plus a watch per composite and claim kind of at most 20 XRDs, in all namespaces, through the shared watch registry, released 5 s after the last user; each claim and composite watch keeps at most 5000 objects (`MAX_ROWS`), and a capped one makes the page and the problems say they show a part. A claim or composite that is not Synced, or not Ready other than creating or deleting, is a problem after 2 minutes; one still being created counts after 30 minutes (a managed database can take a quarter of an hour); a composite a claim stands for is told through its claim; deleting is never a problem.
- **Kyverno.** ClusterPolicies, Policies, `wgpolicyk8s.io` PolicyReports and ClusterPolicyReports, and `reports.kyverno.io` ephemeral reports (listed, never judged: they are Kyverno's intermediate results that end up in the policy reports). A policy shows its readiness (the Ready condition, else the older `status.ready`) and its mode: per rule since Kyverno 1.13 (`validate.failureAction`), else the policy's `validationFailureAction`, Audit by default; rules that differ make it Mixed.
  - **On the object:** every kind's details get "Policy results", read from the one report Kyverno (1.10+) names after the object's uid, in its namespace (a ClusterPolicyReport for a cluster-scoped object) — a watch selected by `metadata.name=<uid>` through the shared registry, never the namespace's whole report list. Older aggregated reports (one per namespace) are not read there.
  - **Per policy:** the reports carrying the policy's label (`cpol.kyverno.io/<name>` or `pol.kyverno.io/<name>`), in all namespaces for a ClusterPolicy and in the policy's namespace for a Policy, at most 1000 reports (the count says when it is incomplete); a name too long for a label gets no summary.
  - **Problems: a new opt-in category `policies`**, not folded into `security`: a failed policy result is a lasting state, an Audit policy can fail on hundreds of objects at once, and Kyverno policies are as often conventions (required labels) as security, so a "Security" label would mislead. With the category on, the PolicyReports and ClusterPolicyReports are watched cluster-wide, each watch keeping at most 5000 reports (`MAX_SOURCE_OBJECTS`; a capped source says it is truncated), and each report with failed results is one problem about the object its `scope` names. A result `error` (the rule could not be evaluated: a variable that does not resolve, a failing API call — the PolicyReport API's "could not be evaluated", `pkg/engine/api/rulestatus.go`) is a problem of its own (`PolicyError`), listed and counted by the table filter but not notified: Kyverno itself treats it as unsuccessful (`EngineResponse.IsSuccessful`, the CLI exits 1) but keeps it apart from a violation and blocks admission on it only with `failurePolicy: Fail`, and it says more about the policy than about the object (amended after the 0.18 review). It is off until turned on (Settings → Notifications, or the Problems page's toggle), like `security`, `practices` and `disk`.
- **Agents and extensions get nothing new.** The three categories are in `NEVER_FOR_AGENTS` (`src-tauri/src/mcp/diagnose_answers.rs`): `get_problems` neither offers nor accepts them. Crossplane's problems come from a provider that watches across namespaces, which the agent's read rules could not narrow; Knative and Kyverno follow so the build gives agents nothing new.

## Why

- Watching Revisions for problems was rejected: telling the latest from an old one needs the Configuration anyway, and Knative already says it there.
- Opening a watch per object of the Crossplane chain was rejected for the stream count; reading the chain once per change of the shown object keeps the view current where it matters (a composite's status changes when its resources do).
- Reading CRDs for `categories` was rejected: the XRDs say exactly which kinds are claims and composites, and a managed resource's shape is unambiguous.
- A namespace-wide watch of PolicyReports for one object's results was rejected: Kyverno writes one report per resource, so a namespace can hold thousands.

## Consequences

- A Crossplane installation with more than 20 XRDs shows and judges the claims and composites of the first 20 (by name); the page says so.
- A Kyverno older than 1.10 (aggregated reports) shows no per-object results and no policy summary; its reports are still listed and, with the category on, judged.
- A Kyverno policy summary depends on the policy labels Kyverno puts on reports; if a release stops writing them, the summary says no report holds results.
